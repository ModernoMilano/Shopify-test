### I'm Not Giving It Back

*Invalshoek: warm-romantisch. Beelden: grotendeels bestaande clips, een paar nieuwe shots.*

**Logline.** On a cold night by the canal he lends her his knit, she never returns it, so he buys another and she takes that one too, until the only way to see his own wardrobe again is to move in with her.

**Opening.**

> HER (sung): I'm not giving it back.  
> HIM (rap): My navy knit. One cold night. Gone, just like that.  
> HER (sung): It's soft, and it still feels like you.  
> HIM (rap): So I bought another one. She took that too.  

**Beats.**

- BOTH: Night at the Navigli, cold wind on the footbridge: he takes off his navy knit and puts it round her shoulders; she zips it up to the stand collar and disappears into it, smiling.
- SHE SINGS: Next morning in her own bedroom, alone: his knit far too big on her, sleeves over her hands, knees pulled up; she sings that she is keeping it.
- HE RAPS: His flat: one empty hanger, him shivering in a t-shirt; flatmate Dave leans in the doorway with a new cardboard box and a raised eyebrow; he zips up the replacement in the mirror.
- BOTH: Second date at the candlelit wine bar, line by line: he arrives in the new knit; her fingertips are already on the sleeve, eyebrows up; he sees it coming and holds his collar.
- HE RAPS: Night tram stop: she waves from the tram wearing knit number two; he stands on the platform in shirtsleeves, laughing at himself. Then her wardrobe rail: navy, oatmeal, taupe in a row; his rail: one lonely hanger.
- SHE SINGS: At her green front door she opens in the navy knit, turns back the collar, reads the name inside and calls it hers; then she holds the door wide.
- BOTH: He walks in carrying one bag and his last cardigan; final picture, one shared rail with everything on it, then the two of them walking through the Galleria, her in his oatmeal, him in navy.

**Refrein-idee.** I'm not giving it back, it's mine now, it's true, / It's soft and it's warm and it feels like you.

**Einde.** He stops fighting it: "Fine. Keep the knits. I'm moving in with them." One wardrobe, two people, both in knitwear in the Galleria.

**Merknaam.** Kept secret until the door scene (about line 19): she turns the collar and sings "It says Moderno Milano. I say mine." Second and last time as the closing duet line.

### Papa, This Is Him

*Invalshoek: warm-romantisch. Beelden: nieuwe beelden nodig.*

**Logline.** Sunday lunch at her parents' flat: her father, a retired Milanese tailor who has judged men by their cloth for forty years, says nothing for the whole meal, then walks round the table and pinches his sleeve.

**Opening.**

> HER (sung): Papa, this is him.  
> HIM (rap): Her father's a tailor. Forty years. He doesn't grin.  
> HER (sung): Be nice, he's the one I chose.  
> HIM (rap): He shook my hand, then looked straight at my clothes.  

**Beats.**

- BOTH: The doorway of her parents' Milan apartment: she presents him proudly; the father, silver moustache, glasses pushed up on his forehead, shakes his hand and looks at the collar, not the face.
- HE RAPS: Flash back to that morning: he tries on outfit after outfit at the mirror, flatmate Dave shakes his head at the hoodie from the couch; he zips the navy knit to the collar and Dave finally nods.
- HE RAPS: The long lunch table: silence, the father cutting bread very slowly with his eyes on the knit; a drop of red sauce falls from the fork and he catches it in his napkin a centimetre from his chest.
- SHE SINGS: She squeezes his hand under the table and sings to her father what he cannot say himself: he is kind, he listens, he makes her laugh; the mother smiles into her glass.
- BOTH: The father stands, walks round the table, pinches the sleeve between two fingers, turns the ribbed collar, checks a seam; the whole table holds its breath. "Who made this?" He answers. One slow nod.
- HE RAPS: The father pulls a chair next to him and pours from the good bottle; the two men lean together talking fabric while she and her mother trade a look across the table.
- BOTH: Next Sunday the door opens and the father stands there in the same knit in oatmeal, arms wide, hugging him before she is even inside; she laughs: now he calls him more than he calls her.

**Refrein-idee.** Papa, this is him, look past the nerves, look in, / He's gentle and he's mine: Papa, this is him.

**Einde.** Acceptance without a speech: the strictest judge in Milan turns up wearing the same knit and hugs him first. She gets her wish, he gets a second father.

**Merknaam.** Kept secret until the father's question (about line 17): "Who made this?" / "Moderno Milano." Second and last time as the final duet line: "Papa, this is him. Moderno Milano."

**Pijnpunt.** Meeting her father: the fear of being weighed and found too light before you have said a word. The clothes do the first talking, and approval comes from the one person who cannot be fooled about quality. Bonus: the father is a second viewer to identify with, so it speaks to an older buyer as well.

**Toon.** Warm family comedy with real tension in the silence, then a big-hearted release; respectful to the father, who is strict but never unkind.

**Beelden.** New picture set, about the full 300 credits: a new father and mother, the parents' doorway, the lunch table, the sleeve inspection, the second Sunday. Only the morning beat reuses existing clips (bathroom or bedroom mirror, the navy zip, Dave on the couch with his spoon, hallway mirror with keys), and she keeps her existing look in the camel coat.

- Score songwriter: haak 9, één helder verhaal 9, duet 8, merk 10, nieuw 9, totaal 45. Risico: Real tension, a clear emotional turn, and a tailor's nod is the most credible quality proof possible. The father's line is a third voice, and she sings to her father more than to him, so the two leads rarely answer each other. Full new picture set.
- Score marketeer: haak 8, één helder verhaal 9, duet 7, merk 9, nieuw 10, totaal 43. Risico: Real tension in one room and a reveal that works as quality proof without a single selling line. The main brand risk: a Milanese tailor approving an 'Italian-inspired' knit invites 'where is it made?' comments, so the father must only nod, never praise. She mostly sings to her father, so the duet between the two leads is thinner. Full 300 credits and two new characters.
- Top zes van de songwriter: The only pitch with real suspense: silence at the table, then a tailor pinching the sleeve. The chorus is a plea a listener can hum, the fear of meeting her father is universal, and the reveal answers the one question a tailor would ask. It also gives an older buyer someone to identify with. Full new picture set. **Verbetering:** Keep it to two voices: the father never speaks, she sings his question for him ('He turns your collar and asks who made it') and he raps the answer. Fold the 'pours the good bottle' beat into the nod so the song has six beats.
- Top zes van de marketeer: The most tension in one room (43): every man knows the fear of being weighed before he speaks, and the silent father keeps people watching for the verdict. 'Who made this?' is the most natural place a brand name can land. It also speaks to an older buyer. **Verbetering:** Make the lunch-table beat a whispered line-by-line exchange between the two of them (his panic, her reassurance under the table), so it is a duet between him and her, not her singing to her father. Keep the father to one nod and no praise of quality.

### Not Marco

*Invalshoek: scrollstopper-twist. Beelden: nieuwe beelden nodig.*

**Logline.** A woman on a blind date sits down at the wrong man's table, he is too stunned to correct her, and both keep the same secret all evening for the same reason.

**Opening.**

> HIM (rap): She sat down at my table and called me by another man's name.  
> HER (sung): Navy knit, window seat, just like he said, so I came.  
> HIM (rap): I should've said "wrong guy". I said "hi". That one's on me.  
> BOTH: One glass, one hour. Who am I supposed to be?  

**Beats.**

- HE RAPS: A wine bar on the Navigli, window seat, alone in the navy knit after a long week. A woman in a camel coat sits down smiling: "Marco?" He opens his mouth and only "hi" comes out.
- SHE SINGS: She almost turned around at the door; it is her third blind date this year. She expected to be bored, and this man asks questions and actually listens.
- BOTH: Line by line across the table. "Your profile says you sail." "I get seasick on the tram." She laughs until the glasses shake; he thinks "tell her", she thinks "he's funnier than his messages".
- SHE SINGS: Her phone lights up on the table: "Marco: so sorry, can't make it tonight." She looks at the screen, looks at him, and turns the phone face down.
- HE RAPS: He cannot carry it any longer. He rehearses in the window reflection, puts his glass down: "I have to tell you something. I'm not Marco."
- SHE SINGS: She turns the phone back over and slides it across: "I know. For forty minutes." They both burst out laughing.
- BOTH: They walk out along the canal. He asks how she picked his table; she says what Marco told her to look for. Then she asks his real name.

**Refrein-idee.** I'm not Marco (HIM) / I know, I know (HER) / / Wrong name, right table, please don't go (BOTH).

**Einde.** The twist is one line: she has known since the text came in and stayed anyway. The last picture is the two of them walking away as she finally asks his name.

**Merknaam.** Twice. On the walk, as the description she was given: "He said: navy knit, Moderno Milano." Then the last line, BOTH: "Wrong man, right table. Moderno Milano."

**Pijnpunt.** The quiet belief "someone like her would never sit down with someone like me". He dressed well for nobody that evening and got treated as the man she was hoping for. Her side: dating fatigue, people who never match their profile, and the relief of someone who listens.

**Toon.** Romantic comedy in one room, light and witty; the tension is a secret the viewers share with him, then with her.

**Beelden.** Same two characters and the wine bar already exist, and the closing Galleria walk could replace the canal walk at no cost. But the song plays almost entirely at one table, so it needs a new set of table shots: her sitting down, the two-shots, the phone with Marco's text, the phone face down, his reflection in the window. No hoodie and no transformation in this one.

- Score songwriter: haak 9, één helder verhaal 8, duet 10, merk 7, nieuw 10, totaal 44. Risico: The chorus is built as an answer ('I'm not Marco' / 'I know, I know'), so the voices truly need each other, and nothing in it resembles the old stories. He lets her believe a lie for most of the song, which strains 'honest and kind'. It is dialogue-heavy for 150 words and the clothes are only an identifier. New table shots needed.
- Score marketeer: haak 9, één helder verhaal 7, duet 9, merk 5, nieuw 10, totaal 40. Risico: The strongest scroll-stopper line of all twelve, but the brand is incidental: nobody describes a blind date by brand name, so the reveal feels pasted on. He lets her believe he is someone else for forty minutes, which rubs against 'honest and kind'. One table for 110 seconds gives monotonous pictures, and there is almost no pain point.
- Top zes van de songwriter: The best true call and response: the chorus only works with both voices, and the story has a clean emotional turn when she turns the phone face down. No hoodie, no makeover, no ex, so it is the furthest from what he already has, and the opening line is a scroll-stopper. New table shots needed. **Verbetering:** Protect his honesty: he starts 'I'm not...' in the first verse and she talks straight over him, then tries again and the glasses arrive, so he is a man who keeps failing to get the truth out, not a man who is hiding it.

### You'll Grow Into It

*Invalshoek: pijnpunt-humor. Beelden: nieuwe beelden nodig.*

**Logline.** Thirty years old and still wearing what his mother once bought 'one size up', a man gets the truth sung to him by his sister at Sunday lunch, and the next Sunday the whole table goes quiet.

**Opening.**

> HIM (rap): Mama bought it big. Said: "You'll grow into it."  
> HER (sung): You're thirty now, brother. You outgrew it.  
> HIM (rap): It's comfy. It's loyal. It's part of who I am.  
> HER (sung): It's got your school name on it. Come on, man.  

**Beats.**

- BOTH: Sunday lunch in his mother's Milan flat, an orange tram passing the window. He sits in a faded school hoodie; his sister across the table sings each truth back at his excuses, line by line; their mother piles more pasta on his plate and pinches his cheek.
- HE RAPS: His wardrobe at home, shot like a museum tour. Every item has a year: the hoodie from 2011, the trainers from a school trip, the jeans Mama hemmed. 'My closet is a museum and I'm the only visitor.'
- SHE SINGS: The sister alone on the balcony. She watched him get the job, the flat, become the one everybody calls when something breaks. She is proud of him. 'You grew up in every way. Your clothes just never got the news.'
- HE RAPS: Café counter on Monday morning. The barista smiles and asks 'Student discount?' He says: 'I'm the manager.' A long, quiet look at his own reflection in the pastry case.
- HE RAPS: He bags the hoodies and keeps one, for Mama. A box arrives. Oatmeal knitted set, ribbed stand collar, slim zip, tailored trousers, suede loafers. In the mirror he stands taller. Her voice floats over it: 'there you are'.
- BOTH: Next Sunday. He walks in and Mama drops the wooden spoon. She fixes his collar, which does not need fixing, checks the label like mothers do and reads it out loud: 'Moderno Milano.' Then she asks: 'Who is she?' He: 'There's no she, Mama. It's just me.' The sister sings: 'He grew into it.'
- BOTH: Last picture: Mama still sends him home with leftovers in a plastic tub. Brother and sister walk to the tram, she bumps his shoulder, and they sing the last chorus together.

**Refrein-idee.** You'll grow into it, Mama always knew / / Took you thirty years, but look at you.

**Einde.** No girl and no ex. He did it for himself, his sister is proud, and his mother still treats him like her boy, only now a well-dressed one. The joke 'Who is she?' / 'It's just me' is the emotional payoff.

**Merknaam.** Twice. First Mama reads the neck label out loud while fixing his collar in beat 6. Then the very last line, both voices: 'Moderno Milano.' The opening is only the hoodie and Mama's sentence, with no brand.

**Pijnpunt.** Dressing like a teenager at thirty. His life grew up (job, flat, responsibility) but his wardrobe stopped at school, so the world keeps treating him as the kid: student discount, cheek pinches. Psychology: self-image catching up with reality, and changing for yourself instead of for a woman. Sisters, mothers and girlfriends will recognise it too and may share it.

**Toon.** Family comedy with heart: an Italian Sunday lunch, a teasing sister, a mother who never stops mothering. His rap is dry and self-deprecating, her singing is soulful. Nobody is mocked; everybody loves him.

**Beelden.** New scenes are needed: the mother's dining room, the sister, the balcony, the café counter and the second Sunday. That is roughly the full 300 credits. If the same male character is kept, the bin bag, the box arriving, the mirror shots and the tram can probably be reused from the existing 65 clips. This is judged from the clip list, not from viewing the clips.

- Score marketeer: haak 8, één helder verhaal 7, duet 7, merk 9, nieuw 10, totaal 41. Risico: Very fresh, with no girl to win, and Mama reading the label is a reveal people will remember and share. **Beelden.** New scenes are needed: the split screen with the invitation, the sofa scroll, the tight blazer, the school gate with lights, the steps. That is about 300 credits. This is judged from the clip list, not from viewing the clips.

- Score marketeer: haak 7, één helder verhaal 8, duet 9, merk 7, nieuw 9, totaal 40. Risico: Comparison with peers is a real, under-used pain point for men of 28-40, and the parallel structure gives the best true duet of the set. ### Be Honest

*Invalshoek: pijnpunt-humor. Beelden: grotendeels bestaande clips, een paar nieuwe shots.*

**Logline.** A single man asks his oldest friend to finally tell him why no first date ever becomes a second, and she tells him the truth she has been holding back for years, lovingly and without mercy.

**Opening.**

> HIM (rap): Be honest. Why am I still alone?  
> HER (sung): Honest? Sit down. Put away your phone.  
> HIM (rap): Is it my jokes? Too short? Too plain?  
> HER (sung): Your jokes are gold. It's the hoodie with the stain.
